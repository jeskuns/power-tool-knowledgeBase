C22.2 No. 147-15
Motor-operated gardening appliances
1 Scope
1.1
This Standard applies to motor-operated gardening appliances* intended for use on nominal system
voltages of 250 V and less and in nonhazardous locations, and designed to be used in accordance with
the Rules of the Canadian Electrical Code, Part I.
*For convenience, the short terms "appliance", "equipment", and "machine" are used herein.
1.2
This Standard applies to cord-connected and battery-operated lawn mowers of the reel and rotary
walking types, edgers, trimmers, edger trimmers, grass shears, cultivators, shredder baggers, gardening
appliances with a blower, leaf blower/vacuums, snow movers, chain saws, and similar equipment
including electric starter motors for gardening appliances.
1.3
This Standard does not apply to sprayers, foggers, and other specific appliances that are covered by
individual Standards of the Canadian Electrical Code, Part II.
1.4
This Standard also includes requirements for cord-connected, double-insulated gardening appliances
(see Clause 7).
1.5
In this Standard, “shall” is used to express a requirement, i.e., a provision that the user is obliged to
satisfy in order to comply with the standard; “should” is used to express a recommendation or that
which is advised but not required; and “may” is used to express an option or that which is permissible
within the limits of the Standard.
Notes accompanying clauses do not include requirements or alternative requirements; the purpose of a
note accompanying a clause is to separate from the text explanatory or informative material.
Notes to tables and figures are considered part of the table or figure and may be written as
requirements.
Annexes are designated normative (mandatory) or informative (non-mandatory) to define their
application.

3 Definitions
The following definitions shall apply in this Standard:
Attended appliance — an appliance that
- a)
has a momentary contact switch;
- b)
is designed to be hand held when in use; or
- c)
would only be left in the ON position during normal use if the operator were in the immediate
vicinity.
Battery-operated appliance — an appliance powered by a rechargeable or nonrechargeable battery.
Cord-connected appliance — an appliance that is connected to the electrical supply by a cord set or a
power supply cord that terminates in a suitable attachment plug.
Double-insulated appliance — an appliance in which double insulation is used throughout, except for
parts where, because double insulation is manifestly impracticable, reinforced insulation is used.
Note: Insulating material may form a part or the whole of the enclosure of a double insulated appliance.

Double insulation — an insulation system comprising functional insulation and supplementary
insulation, with the two insulations physically separate and so arranged that they are not
simultaneously subjected to the same degree of deteriorating influences (e.g., temperature,
contaminants, etc).
Functional insulation — insulation necessary for the proper functioning of the appliance and for basic
protection against electric shock.
Hand-held appliance — an appliance intended to be held and supported in the hand during normal use
whose motor forms an integral part of the appliance.

Normal load — a load that approximates as closely as possible the most severe conditions of normal
use without being a deliberate overload.
Operator zone (illustrated in Figures 1 and 2) — the area within the circle that is located to the rear of
the mower on its longitudinal centreline behind the nearest blade tip circle.
Note: An appliance with a swing-over handle has two operator zones since the mower cuts while moving in either
direction.
Reinforced insulation — an improved functional insulation with such mechanical and electrical qualities
that it provides the same degree of protection against electric shock as double insulation.
Note: An enclosure of insulating material may form a part or the whole of the reinforced insulation.
Saw chain pole pruner — a portable, hand-held electric motor-operated gardening appliance with a
saw chain cutting attachment having a guide bar no more than 25 cm (10 in) in length mounted on a
pole to enable an operator to prune branches from a distance.
Supplementary insulation — an independent insulation provided in addition to the functional insulation
in order to ensure protection against electric shock in case of the failure of the functional insulation.
Note: An enclosure of insulating material may form a part or the whole of the supplementary insulation.
4 Construction
4.1 General requirements
General requirements applicable to this Standard are given in CAN/CSA-C22.2 No. 0, General
Requirements—Canadian Electrical Code, Part II.
4.2 General
4.2.1
Components, that are provided as part of an appliance, and auxiliary electric equipment shall conform
to that Standard of the Canadian Electrical Code, Part II, that covers such components and shall be
suitable for the intended application.

4.2.2
Accessories supplied with or recommended for use with an appliance shall be specifically approved for
the use intended or shall be investigated as an integral part of the appliance.
4.2.3
Appliances shall withstand the physical abuse test of Clause 6.14.
4.2.4
Appliances shall be tested and comply with the normal temperature test of Clause 6.5 If the conditions
of normal operation do not also represent the abnormal conditions likely to be encountered in actual
service, equipment shall not become a hazard when operated under such abnormal conditions and shall
be tested and comply with the abnormal temperature test of Clause 6.17.
4.2.5
Appliances shall be tested as specified in Clause 6.3 and be capable of starting and operating normally
on branch circuits protected by ordinary fuses (not time delay) that have current ratings corresponding
to those of the branch circuits to which the appliances would be connected.
4.2.6
Appliances shall be tested and comply with the rating test of Clause 6.4.
4.3 Enclosures
4.3.1
Electrical parts of appliances, except power supply cords, shall be so located or enclosed that protection
against accidental contact with live parts, either bare or insulated, is provided. Parts of outer enclosures
that are intended to be removed by the user of the appliance without the use of tools (to permit the
attachment of accessories, for making operating adjustments, or for other reasons) shall not be
considered a suitable means of protection against shock hazard unless the supply cord must be
removed from the appliance before disassembly. Insulated brush caps do not require additional
enclosures.

6 Tests
6.1 General
6.1.1 Sequence of tests
Except as otherwise indicated, one representative sample shall be subjected to each test described in
Clauses 6.3 to 6.19. The order of the tests shall correspond to the order of these Clauses.
6.1.2 Test voltage
The test voltage, unless otherwise specified, shall be the nominal system voltage (e.g., 120 V) for the
supply system on which the equipment is intended to operate. The nominal system voltage is that
specified in CSA CAN3-C235. The test voltage shall be supplied to a 30 m long cord set, or equivalent
impedance. For a general-use cord set this shall be No. 16 AWG size cord, except that appliances rated
over 13 A and up to 15 A shall use a No. 14 AWG size cord. For a special-use cord set this may be No. 18
AWG for appliances rated not over 10 A.

6.1.3 Rated frequency
Equipment shall be tested at 60 Hz unless rated for dc only, or at the marked frequency when other
than 60 Hz.
6.2 Normal load
6.2.1 General
6.2.1.1
Loads that have been found to be representative of the normal load are indicated below for some of
the most common types of appliances.
Note: Appliances with characteristics not covered by these test procedures may be tested as necessary to meet the
intent of these requirements.
6.2.1.2
Appliances having a self-propelled feature shall have an equivalent load added either by loading the
drive wheels or by increasing the load on the cutting blades.
6.2.1.3
Appliances using various attachments (e.g., a mower having a mulching attachment) shall be tested with
the attachment(s) that provides the greatest maximum normal load.
6.2.2 Mowers
The input of mowers shall be determined under no load conditions.
6.2.3 Grass shears, edgers, trimmers, and edger trimmers
The input of grass shears, edgers, trimmers, and edger trimmers shall be determined with the appliance
operating continuously at no load, unless the manufacturer requests tests under representative load
conditions.
6.2.4 Cultivators
The input of cultivators shall be determined by using dry 60–80 core sand. The cultivator shall be tested
at 80% of the effective length of the blade, or as specified in the manufacturer's instructions.
6.2.5 Snow movers
The input of snow movers shall be determined while the appliance is stationary and is moving water
that is 50 mm above the bottom of the impeller.
6.2.6 Chain saws and saw chain pole pruners
The input of chain saws and saw chain pole pruners shall be determined by cross cutting a soft pine
beam, the thickness of which shall be not less than 150% of the chain saw bar width. The width shall be
75% of the length of the bar. The bar length shall be considered to be the effective bar length on which
the chain rides. The bar width shall be considered to be the bar width plus twice the chain thickness.
6.2.7 Starter motor for gasoline engines
The input of a starter motor shall be determined by cranking the motor for which it is intended for 5 s,
at normal ambient temperature, and at an ambient temperature of –20°C, after it has been conditioned

6.6 Dielectric strength
6.6.1
Immediately after the temperature test, the equipment shall withstand at room temperature for a
period of 1 min, without breakdown, the application of an ac voltage of 50 or 60 Hz between live parts
and non-current-carrying conductive parts that may become grounded, as follows:
- a)
1000 V for machines of any rated voltage from 31 to 250 V; and
- b)
500 V for extra-low voltage circuits (other than self-generating millivolt circuits) at 30 V or less.
Solid state control circuits, which normally have a connection to ground, may have the ground
connection disconnected during the dielectric test.
6.6.2
An appliance that can cut into or contact concealed live wires shall withstand without breakdown for
one minute the application of a 60 Hz essentially sinusoidal potential of 1250 V between the insulating
grasping surfaces of the appliance and accessible dead metal surfaces which may become energized.
6.6.3
Compliance with the requirements of Clause 6.6.1 shall be determined by means of a suitable testing
transformer, the output of which can be regulated. Starting at zero, the applied voltage shall be
increased gradually and at a uniform rate either until the required test value is reached or breakdown
occurs.
6.6.4
For this test, all switches and controls shall be in the ON position.

6.9 Performance of manually operated switches
6.9.1 Overload
6.9.1.1
Switches shall be tested at the specified test voltage (see Clause 6.1.2) and the rated frequency of the
appliance, by making and breaking the stalled motor current 50 consecutive times without undue
arcing, burning, pitting, or welding of the switch contacts, and without either electrical or mechanical
failure. If, in addition to the motor, another circuit is controlled by the switch, it shall also be included in
the test load.

6.9.1.2
The following test conditions shall be complied with:
- a)
The duration of the ON and OFF periods shall be such that the temperature rise of the motor
windings does not exceed that normally encountered in service. The switches shall not be operated
at a rate faster than 10 times per minute.
- b)
Those switch poles that are least liable to arc to non-current-carrying metal parts shall be
connected to such parts through a suitable fuse. Under this condition the fuse shall not open.
6.9.1.3
If the equipment has both an ac and a dc rating, the test specified in Clause 6.9.1.1 shall be conducted
with the equipment connected to a dc supply of the maximum rated voltage, and the dc overload test
shall be considered representative of the test for ac. In the event that the switches fail to pass the dc
tests but subsequent samples pass the ac tests, the equipment shall be rated for ac use only.
6.9.2 Endurance
Switches shall comply with the endurance test required by CSA C22.2 Nos. 55 or 111, as applicable,
except that the rated load may be considered to be the load controlled by the switch in the equipment.
6.10 Stability
The stability of a mower or snow mover shall be such that all wheels or skids will maintain their position
on a surface inclined 15° from the horizontal. A mower that is intended to be operated with or without
a grass catcher shall be tested with and without the catcher attached.
6.11 Flame test
Plastic enclosures and supports that are required to have flame retardant coatings, liners, etc, to meet
the requirements of the flame test shall be tested with the parts, or a specimen, as required, in both
the received (new) condition and (with additional samples) after the following conditioning:
- a)
Samples shall be placed in a circulating air oven for 100 h at the specified continuous temperature
rating of the plastic as determined by ASTM D648 at 66 psi or at 10°C above the maximum
temperature occurring on the material during the normal temperature test in Clause 6.5,
whichever is the lower; but in no case shall this temperature be less than 90°C.
After the heating cycle the samples shall be removed from the oven and placed for 30 min in a
refrigerator held at a temperature of –30 ± 2°C.
- b)
The heating and cooling cycles in Item (a) shall be repeated twice more.
- c)
After the third heating and cooling cycle the samples shall be conditioned for 24 h at 32 ± 2°C and
a relative humidity of 80–90%.

10 cycles/min unless the manufacturer agrees to a faster rate. The flexing test may be performed on a
different sample than the one used for the strain relief test.

7 Requirements for cord-connected double-insulated appliances
7.1 General
7.1.1
This Clause is supplementary to, or amendatory of, those requirements given elsewhere in this
Standard. It forms the basis for the design and construction of cord-connected appliances that have
double insulation in lieu of the grounding of exposed non-current-carrying conductive parts, if any, and
that are marked to indicate this in accordance with Clause 7.4.

<!-- 第 47 页 -->
Copyrighted material licensed to Intertek. No further reproduction or distribution permitted.
7.1.2
These requirements shall apply to cord-connected equipment on circuits not exceeding 150 V to ground
for use in nonhazardous locations, and designed to be used in accordance with the Rules of the
Canadian Electrical Code, Part I.
7.2 General requirements
7.2.1
Double-insulated appliances shall not have provision for grounding.
7.2.2
The construction shall be such that the failure of two independent sections of insulation must occur
before any exposed metal can become alive, unless the construction is such that reinforced insulation is
acceptable (see the definition of “double-insulated appliance” in Clause 3).
7.2.3
Reinforced insulation shall be used only when it is manifestly impracticable to use double insulation.
7.2.4
Supplementary insulation shall provide insulating and mechanical properties that are not less than
those of the functional insulation normally acceptable for the appliance.
7.2.5
Where a part or the whole of the enclosure is of insulating material (i.e., there is no exposed metal), it
shall be treated as supplementary insulation or reinforced insulation.
7.2.6
Functional insulation and metal insulated from bare live parts by functional insulation shall not be
accessible to the user, as required by Clause 7.3.2.2.
7.2.7
Capacitors shall not be connected to exposed conductive parts.
7.2.8
The construction shall be such that normal use of the appliance, including servicing, will not result in the
loss of insulation as originally provided.
7.3 Construction
7.3.1 General
7.3.1.1
Except where reinforced insulation is used, supplementary insulation shall be interposed between all
exposed non-current-carrying conductive parts and
- a)
functionally insulated current-carrying parts including insulated wire; and

<!-- 第 48 页 -->
Copyrighted material licensed to Intertek. No further reproduction or distribution permitted.
- b)
non-current-carrying conductive parts that are separated from bare current-carrying parts by
functional insulation.
Note: A permanently maintained air space may be accepted as either supplementary or reinforced insulation.
7.3.1.2
The construction shall be such that the added protection provided by any additional insulation will not
be reduced by the normal use and servicing to which the appliance is liable to be subjected.
7.3.1.3
Unless protected from physical abuse by the outer enclosure of the appliance or by other means,
supplementary insulation shall be adequately resistant to such abuse.
Note: Supplementary insulation that comprises the outer enclosure of the appliance is considered to be adequately
resistant to physical abuse if the appliance complies with the mechanical strength requirements of Clause 6.14.
7.3.1.4
At a switch having its own enclosure of insulating material, reinforced insulation shall be acceptable in
place of double insulation if all of the following conditions are met:
- a)
all live parts of the switch, other than terminals, shall be completely enclosed in the switch
enclosure;
- b)
no dead metal part that extends outside the switch enclosure shall enter the arc chamber;
- c)
a plunger, toggle, or the like that contacts live parts shall be made wholly of insulating material;
- d)
with the exposed external parts of the plunger, toggle, or the like removed, no live part inside the
switch enclosure shall be accessible, and no live part inside the switch enclosure shall be contacted
by the actuating arm, cam, or the like;
- e)
the metal mounting screws or rivets by which the switch is secured to the accessible dead metal of
the tool shall not pass through the body of the switch enclosure but may pass through tabs, ears,
or other projections from the switch body, including a piece of insulating material secured to the
switch; and
- f)
a portion of a switch or switch enclosure that contains arcing parts and that may therefore be
subjected to arcing shall be separated from the exposed dead metal of the appliance by mica not
less than 0.127 mm thick or by other insulation having equivalent properties. Metal switch
assembly screws, rivets, clamps, or other devices that pass through or around the body of the
switch enclosure shall be suitably insulated from the dead metal to which the switch is secured.
7.3.2 Openings and recesses
7.3.2.1
There shall be no openings other than those that are required for the proper functioning of the
appliance, including the dissipation of the heat generated during its normal operation.
7.3.2.2
Openings or recesses that allow access to bare live parts, functionally insulated parts, or functional
insulation shall be of such size or shape that they will prevent the entry of Probe 1 as shown in Figure 4,
inserted to only length L.
Note: Magnet (e.g., film coated) wire is considered to be a bare live part for the purposes of this requirement.

7.3.2.3
Openings, including construction joints, shall be designed to minimize the accumulation of dirt or
fragments of metal and commutator dust, which could cause bridging or serious contamination of
insulating surfaces.
7.3.3 Mechanical assembly
7.3.3.1
Bare live parts shall be so constructed and secured that in normal use they cannot come into contact
with supplementary insulation or non-current-carrying conductive parts that are separated from
external conductive parts by only supplementary insulation.
7.3.3.2
The construction shall be such that an air gap between such parts cannot be reduced to below the value
permitted or easily bridged by foreign matter.
7.3.3.3
The construction shall be such that it will prevent wires, screws, nuts, washers, springs, and similar
parts, which might become loose or free, from bridging supplementary or reinforced insulation. Only
one part shall be considered as loosening or becoming free at any one time.
7.3.3.4
Sleeves, if provided as supplementary insulation on insulated conductors, shall be secured in position to
prevent their accidental removal (e.g., during servicing).
7.3.3.5
An accessible brush cap shall be
- a)
of suitable insulating material;
- b)
recessed so that its top is behind the plane of the brush holder opening in the surrounding portion
of the enclosure of the appliance; and
- c)
capable of withstanding three impact tests as specified in Clause 6.14.3, each impact being made
on a different part or area of the enclosure, without cracking or exposing bare live parts.
7.3.4 Supply connection
7.3.4.1
The power supply cord shall have a mechanical serviceability of at least that required for grounded
appliances, and shall not have a grounding conductor.
7.3.4.2
The power supply cord shall be additionally insulated from exposed conductive parts at the point where
it enters the appliance.
Note: A jacket on a cord is not considered as supplementary insulation.
7.3.4.3
A metal strain relief clamp shall not be accessible and shall not make contact with an accessible non-
current-carrying conductive part unless the clamp is suitably insulated from the cord.

7.3.5.1
The spacings shall be not less than those specified in Table 4, except that larger spacings may be

7.3.5.2
Magnet (i.e., film coated) wire shall be considered a bare live part in determining compliance with the

7.3.5.3
When spacings are measured, screws that are not prevented from loosening shall be positioned, by

7.5.2 Leakage current
7.5.2.1
When tested under the conditions specified in Clauses 7.5.2.2 to 7.5.2.4, the leakage current, measured
with a resistance of 1500 Ω connected in the circuit and with the appliance at normal operating
temperature, shall not exceed
- a)
3.5 mA with the nominal system voltage specified in CSA CAN3-C235 (e.g., 120 V) applied between
each side of the supply circuit, in turn, and the inaccessible functionally insulated non-current-
carrying conductive parts; and
- b)
0.5 mA with the nominal system voltage applied between each side of the supply circuit, in turn,
and the exposed non-current-carrying conductive parts, or metal foil 10 x 20 cm in area or smaller
if total area is less than 200 cm2, that are in contact with the external nonconductive surfaces.
7.5.2.2
Prior to measuring the leakage current, the sample shall be operated under no load conditions for 100 h
or until the brushes wear out, whichever interval is shorter, but in no case for less than 25 h.
For a double-insulated starter motor, the motor shall be operated under no load at a duty cycle of 5 s
ON and 3.5 min OFF, or a shorter OFF time at the discretion of the manufacturer, for a total ON time
of 1 h.
Note: The brushes may be replaced during this test.
7.5.2.3
Following the no load (conditioning) test specified in Clause 7.5.2.2 and prior to measuring the leakage
current, the sample shall be subjected for 48 h to a moist atmosphere having a relative humidity of
80–90% at a temperature of 32 ± 2°C.
7.5.2.4
The test frequency shall be rated hertz or dc, whichever is applicable.
7.5.3 Supplementary insulation test
7.5.3.1
Severe overstress of the functional insulation shall not cause the failure of the supplementary insulation
as determined by
- a)
the leakage current test specified in Clause 7.5.2.1(b) monitored while the unit is still hot; and
- b)
the dielectric strength test specified in Clause 7.5.3.4. Nor shall severe overstress cause bare or
functionally insulated live parts to be exposed, as specified in Clause 7.3.2.
7.5.3.2
To determine compliance with the requirements of Clause 7.5.3.1, a motor-operated appliance shall be
subjected to a running overload test. While it is connected to a fused 30 A branch circuit of nominal
system voltage, by operating the appliance at full load for a period of 30 min and increasing the load by
10% every 30 min until any one of the following conditions occurs:
- a)
the 30 A fuse blows;
- b)
the input current suddenly increases by 100%, or more, of rated current;
- c)
flame appears (if flame occurs it shall be extinguished immediately); or
- d)
there is an open circuit in the motor winding(s).

7.5.3.3 Protector
7.5.3.3.1
During the test specified in Clause 7.5.3.2, any protective device, provided with the appliance, that can
be reset, replaced, repaired, or otherwise modified by the user, shall be short-circuited.
Note: The objective of the test is to determine the integrity of the appliance insulation and not the effectiveness of

7.5.3.3.2
A one-shot nonresettable or nonreplaceable thermal cutoff that is permanently embedded in the motor

temperature. The appliance shall then be capable of withstanding, without breakdown, for a period of
1 min, the application of an ac voltage of 1000 V between the live parts and the accessible non-current-
carrying conductive parts or metal foil as specified in Clause 6.7.7.

Annex A
Safe operation practices for walk-behind mowers
Note: This Appendix is not a mandatory part of this Standard.
A.1 . Safety instructions
A.1.1 Training
The following should be carried out for training:
- a)
Read the operating and service instruction manual carefully. Be thoroughly familiar with the
controls and the proper use of the equipment.
- b)
Never allow children to operate a power mower.
- c)
Keep the area of operation clear of all persons, particularly small children, and pets.
- d)
Do not use a mower for any job except that for which it is intended.
A.1.2 Preparation
The following preparations should be carried out:
- a)
Thoroughly inspect the area where the equipment is to be used and remove all stones, sticks,
wires, bones, and other foreign objects.
- b)
Do not operate the equipment when barefoot or wearing open sandals. Always wear substantial
footwear.
- c)
Disengage the self-propelled mechanism or drive clutch on units so equipped before starting the
motor.
- d)
Never attempt to make a wheel height adjustment while the motor is running.
- e)
Mow only in daylight or in good artificial light.
- f)
Never operate the equipment on wet grass. Always be sure of your footing; keep a firm hold on
the handle and walk, never run.
A.1.3 Operation
During operation, the following should be observed:
- a)
Do not put hands or feet near or under rotating parts. Keep clear of the discharge opening at all
times.
- b)
Stop the blade(s) when crossing gravel drives, walks, or roads.
- c)
After striking a foreign object, stop the motor, unplug, thoroughly inspect the mower for any
damage, and repair the damage before restarting and operating the mower.
- d)
If the equipment should start to vibrate abnormally, stop the motor and check immediately for the
cause. Vibration is generally a warning of trouble.
- e)
Stop the motor and unplug whenever leaving the equipment, before cleaning the mower housing,
and before making any repairs or inspections.
- f)
Shut the motor off and wait until the blade comes to a complete stop before removing the grass
catcher or before unclogging the chute.
- g)
Mow across the face of slopes, never up and down. Exercise extreme caution when changing
direction on slopes. Do not mow excessively steep slopes.
- h)
Never operate the mower without proper guards, plates, or other safety protective devices in
place.
- i)
Do not wash with a hose; avoid getting water in motor and electrical connections.

A.1.4 Maintenance
The following maintenance items should be carried out:
- a)
Check the blade and the engine mounting bolts at frequent intervals for proper tightness.
- b)
Keep all nuts, bolts, and screws tight to be sure the equipment is in safe working condition.
- c)
Check bag frequently for deterioration and wear, and replace worn bags. Check that replacement
bags comply with the original manufacturer's recommendations or specifications.
- d)
Replace the blade if it is bent or cracked. An unbalanced blade causes vibration that could damage
the motor drive unit or cause personal injury.
- e)
Sharpen a dull blade equally at each end to maintain the balance.
- f)
Clean the blade thoroughly and check its balance by supporting it horizontally on a thin nail
through its centre hole. The nail should also be in a horizontal position. If either end of the blade
rotates downward, remove some metal from the heavy or lower end until the blade is balanced. It
is balanced when neither end drops.

Annex B (normative)
Safety instructions for saw chain pole pruners
Note: This Annex is a mandatory part of this Standard.
B.1
The instruction manual shall include the following minimum information:
- a)
The following warning:
“WARNING – Do not operate near electrical power lines. The unit has not been designed to provide
protection from electric shock in the event of contact with overhead electric lines, Consult local
regulations for safe distances from overhead electric power lines and ensure that the operating
position is safe and secure before operating the saw chain pole pruner.”

- b)
Description and identification of the principal parts of the saw chain pole pruner including the
safety devices and harness, and the use of the quick release mechanism.
- c)
Instruction for adjusting the proper tension of the saw chain, lubricating, maintenance, and for
transportation and storage.
- d)
Advice on correct working posture, maintaining firm footing and balance during operation
including the safe usage of the harness, the quick release mechanism, and the need for rest
periods and changing working position.
- e)
Advice of the need for hearing, head, and eye protection and the necessary PPE to be used
including correct clothing.
- f)
Advice on the risk for bystanders, especially for children, and the need to keep them at a safe
distance during the operation of the saw chain pole pruner.
B.2
The instruction manual shall also include the following safety warnings:
- a)
Keep all parts of the body away from the saw chain. Make sure the switch is off when clearing
jammed material. Saw chain continues to move after the switch is turned off. A moment of
inattention while operating the extended-reach pruner may result in serious personal injury.
- b)
Carry the extended-reach pruner by the handle with the saw chain stopped. When transporting or
storing the extended-reach pruner, always fit the saw chain device cover. Proper handling of the
extended-reach pruner will reduce possible personal injury from the saw chain.
- c)
Hold the extended-reach pruner by insulated gripping surfaces only, because the saw chain may
contact hidden wiring or its own cord. A saw chain contacting a "live" wire may make exposed
metal parts of the extended-reach pruner "live" and could give the operator an electric shock.
- d)
Keep cable away from cutting area. During operation the cable may be hidden in shrubs and can be
accidentally cut by the saw chain.
- e)
Do not use the extended-reach pruner in bad weather conditions, especially when there is a risk of
lightning. This decreases the risk of being struck by lightning.
- f)
To reduce the risk of electrocution, never use near any electrical power lines. Contact with or use
near power lines may cause serious injury or electric shock resulting in death.
