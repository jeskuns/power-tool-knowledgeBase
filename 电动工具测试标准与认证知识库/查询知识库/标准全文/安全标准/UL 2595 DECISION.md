incorporating detachable, integral and separable battery packs. The maximum rated voltage for
appliances and battery packs is 25075 V d.c.

3.26 HAZARDOUS VOLTAGE - Voltage between parts having an average value exceeding 60 V d.c. or
exceeding 42.450 V peak when the peak-to-peak ripple exceeds 10% of the average value. This value is
30 V d.c. or 21.2 V peak when the peak-to-peak ripple exceeds 10% of the average value for those
appliances where the user’s skin is likely to be wet a majority of time in use. See Indent B in Table D1.1.
3.27.1 INSULATION TOUCH CURRENT - upon contact with accessible surface(s) of a battery operated
appliance with a working voltage that is a hazardous voltage, current which ﬂows through a person, where
at least one accessible part is connected to internal circuitry, other than through protective impedance
3.41.1 SWITCHED CIRCUIT - A low-power circuit when the power switch is in the “off” state. Annex B
provides the requirements for a low-power circuit.
3.47 WORKING VOLTAGE - Without the effect of transient voltages, the voltage across any insulation or
between any parts of different polarity when the appliance is supplied by (a) fully charged battery(ies) and
operating at no-load, or with the appliance in the “off” condition, whichever is greater.

16.12 For an appliance having hazardous voltages, it shall be constructed such that if any part becomes
loose or falls out of position, it cannot reduce the creepage distances or clearances to less than 50 % of
the values speciﬁed in Table 20.1. The provision of barriers, or adequately securing the parts, or providing
sufficiently large creepage distances and clearances can be employed to ensure compliance. Only a
single part becoming loose or falling out of position is considered. Spring washers are considered to be
adequate for preventing the loosening of electrical connections. Unless the wires from terminals or
soldered connections are held in place near to the terminal or termination, independent of the terminal

position when the terminal screw is loosened, it shall be regarded as not liable to move away from the
terminal.

RATIONALE FOR DECISION:
Battery-operated appliances with rated voltages over 75 V d.c. are currently not covered under UL 2595.
UL has been approached to investigate battery-operated appliances with rated voltages over 75 V dc. This
Certiﬁcation Requirement Decision is intended to document the additional requirements for evaluating
battery operated appliances up to 250 V d.c. are outlined in this CRD, and are based on requirements that
will be in IEC/UL 62841-1.
These additional requirements include specifying the appropriate creepage and clearances, electric
strength test voltage, outlining tracking requirements to address the risk of ﬁre for circuits that are
energized continuously, even when the power switch is off; and the addition of an insulation touch current
test following moisture resistance conditioning.
With respect to voltages greater than 75 V dc and electric shock, the risk of electric shock for
battery-operated appliances only exists when the user touches both sides of the battery circuit. Insulation
touch current requirements after moisture resistance testing were developed for appliances where one
side of the battery circuit is connected to accessible parts or surfaces other than through protective
impedance, such as the frame. The limits for dc touch current are based on the Standard For Safety For
Leakage Current for Appliances, UL 101 and Standard for Effects of current on human beings and
livestock – Part 1:General aspects IEC 60479-1. The effects of direct current are described in section 6
in IEC 60479-1 and further supported by the rationale in Rationale for Choice of Meter, and Alternative
Meters, paragraph A6.10 of UL 101.
The deﬁnition of hazardous voltage speciﬁes that the voltage between parts having an average value
exceeding 42.4 V peak has been increased to 50 V peak when the ripple exceeds 10 % of the average
value. The increase is in line with the deﬁnition for Safety Extra Low Voltage (see clause 3.49 of UL
62841-1). Since clause 3.49 of UL 62841-1 speciﬁes a value of 50 V rather than 42.4 V for electric circuits
that have galvanic separation from the supplying electric power system, for battery systems, there is no
potential with respect to earth and have systems with a similar condition.
In addition, as battery-operated appliances may not be recognized by the user as a potential electric
shock hazard, a warning marking was added for battery-operated appliances where hazardous voltages
are present.
With respect to Table 20.1, the values are equal or larger than the values required by IEC 60664-1, when
an overvoltage category II, a material group III, a pollution degree 1 for parts protected against deposition
of dirt and for lacquered or enamelled windings, a pollution degree 3 for other parts, inhomogeneous
electric ﬁeld are applied. Areas not protected against deposition of dirt are deﬁned as all points where air
drawn through the appliance (by the fan) can reach, especially areas where deposits of carbon dust, metal
ﬁlings, and wood dust occur. The areas of concern are usually around the brush holders and to the rear
of the commutator.
